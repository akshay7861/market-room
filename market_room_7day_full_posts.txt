TITLE: Geelong Refinery Fire: No Immediate Fuel Curtailment, But Watch Gas and Power Tightness

CATALYST: Viva’s Geelong refinery fire won’t trigger stage three fuel restrictions in Australia for now

CONTENT:
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The fire at Viva’s Geelong refinery is a clear supply shock to one of Australia’s limited refining hubs, but the government’s decision to hold off on escalating fuel restrictions signals that physical product inventories and import flexibility are still cushioning immediate downstream tightness. This keeps refined fuel availability relatively stable in the near term, capping any abrupt spike in domestic gasoline and diesel prices.

The key transmission is through refined product supply chains feeding transport and power generation sectors. If Geelong’s outage extends, regional distributors will tap into inventories or ramp imports, but these buffers are finite. Given the tight linkage between liquid fuels and gas-fired power plants in Australia, any sustained product squeeze could push natural gas demand higher as utilities seek alternatives, underpinning gas prices and tightening the gas-power spread.

This update shifts the prior thesis from anticipating near-term fuel rationing risks to a more cautious watch-and-wait stance on inventory depletion rates and import logistics. The market’s subtle response in natural gas (+0.42% to $2.66/MMBtu) reflects modest concerns about fuel substitution demand, but no panic yet.

The next critical indicator to watch is Australian refined fuel inventory levels and import volumes over the next two weeks. A sharp drawdown or delayed shipments would confirm rising supply risk, likely forcing government intervention and pushing fuels and related gas prices higher, feeding inflationary pressures in transport and industrial sectors.

TITLE: ValOre-Hatchet Uranium Deal Delays Highlight Ongoing Uranium Supply Uncertainty

CATALYST: ValOre Metals extends closing date for Hatchet Uranium Corp. disposition to April 30, pending TSXV approval and disclosure submission.

CONTENT:
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ValOre’s update on the Hatchet Uranium Corp. (HUC) transaction signals a delay in closing, extending the deadline to April 30 and requiring detailed disclosure to the TSX Venture Exchange. While procedural, this postponement keeps a key uranium supply asset in flux, sustaining uncertainty around physical uranium availability. Given Hatchet’s 51% stake held by ValOre, the deal’s completion would clarify ownership and future production potential, potentially unlocking incremental uranium supply into a market already feeling the squeeze from underinvestment and geopolitical supply risks.

This delay matters now as uranium inventories remain tight globally, and any deferment in bringing new supply online sustains upward price pressure. That dynamic feeds through to nuclear fuel costs, which are a non-trivial input into power generation inflation, particularly in regions reliant on nuclear energy. Persistent tightness also supports uranium equities, which are sensitive to supply clarity and project development timelines.

The key transmission channel here is supply visibility influencing uranium price discovery and risk premia, which cascades into energy inflation and broader commodity market sentiment, especially under a tightening inflation regime.

The shift from a near-close to extended due diligence and regulatory review changes the near-term supply outlook from more definitive to conditional, delaying potential de-risking of uranium supply constraints.

Watch TSXV’s acceptance of the transaction and formal closing announcement next. Confirmation of closure would likely ease supply overhang fears and could temper uranium price rallies, while further delays or regulatory hurdles would reinforce current tightness and risk premia.
